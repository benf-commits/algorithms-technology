In the Media

What has been reported, and by whom

Coverage of this research sits first. The wider Australian reporting on schools, student photographs and AI follows. Every item on this page was opened and read before it was listed, and every link goes to the original.

Featured

The Sydney Morning Herald · Emily Kowal · 16 August 2026

‘Disaster waiting to happen’: Parents demand schools revise social media policy

An SMH exclusive reporting that parents are demanding the NSW Department of Education overhaul its social media policy after research showed how easily student photos published by schools can be mass-harvested using AI. The department responded that social media is an “important tool” for schools and that it advises a “risk-based approach”, and said there had been no directive for schools to increase posting.

“Do they not realise that all the photos they publish publicly are so easily data harvested by anyone? It’s a disaster waiting to happen.”

Kara Jade, Newcastle parent, quoted in the article

The audit this reporting draws on is published in full, with its methodology and its limitations, on The Audit. The two government documents in conflict are quoted side by side on Conflicting Advice.

Coverage of this research

Reporting that draws on the audit of NSW government school Facebook and Instagram pages, or on the campaign to change NSW Department of Education procedure PD-2011-0418-01.

The featured piece above is the only coverage of this research so far. Further items will be listed here as they run.

The wider story in Australian media

Australian reporting on schools publishing photographs of students, on imagery harvested from school accounts, on AI-generated abuse of children’s images, and on the government advice and policy that governs any of it. This is a record of what has been published, not an endorsement of it.

28 April 2026
Presenters are unlawfully posting student photos to boost their business: expert

Cybersafety expert Susan McLean warns that lax photo-permission practices are letting external presenters unlawfully publish images of students to promote their businesses, and are exposing schools to legal risk including family court subpoenas where orders protect particular children. A direct critique of permission to publish consent design: McLean argues informed consent must specify each use rather than relying on bundled or yes-or-no forms.

Brigidine College in Brisbane stopped publishing recognisable photographs of students on its official social media accounts, limiting shareable imagery to side profiles, large groups and rear views, to reduce the risk of AI image manipulation. Griffith University criminologist Danielle Harris backs the change and argues other schools should remove identifiable student photographs altogether.

10 February 2025

The Conversation · Karley Beckman and Tiffani Apps, University of Wollongong · 10 February 2025
Written for parents receiving a school permission to publish form. Explains that Australian privacy regulation requires informed consent before a school publishes a student's personal information, including photographs and video, on any online platform, and sets out three questions parents should ask before signing.
10 February 2025
Should NSW schools be sharing photos of students on Facebook? Maybe not
Illawarra Mercury · Tareyn Varley · 10 February 2025
A Wollongong mother ticked the do-not-publish box when enrolling her two children, then found both children's faces in a video on the school's Facebook page because their teachers were unaware of the no-permission status. The Australian Federal Police and the eSafety Commissioner advise caregivers to consider what back-to-school photographs they post and who can see them, warning that publicly visible images and the details they reveal, including school name, uniform and location, can be misused. Addressed to parents rather than to schools.
26 November 2024
The online world isn't always safe. Here's what to know before posting photos of your child
The Conversation · Catherine Jane Archer, Giselle Woodley and Katrin Langton · 26 November 2024
An explainer on the risks and benefits of posting children's photographs online, drawing on Australian survey data. Its school-relevant advice is that parents should review the policies of institutions their child belongs to and check whether those institutions seek children's consent before photographing them.
24 July 2024
Is your child's photo on their school Facebook page? What does this mean for their privacy?
The Conversation · Karley Beckman and Tiffani Apps, University of Wollongong · 24 July 2024
Examines how Australian schools use children's images on social media and school websites, and what the Australian Privacy Principles require of them. Identifies three gaps in school consent practice: no platform-specific consent options, unclear procedures for families who decline, and minimal involvement of the students themselves.
9 July 2024
Photos of Australian kids are being used to train AI, and could a Spotify model give us back control over our data?
ABC News · Ange Lavoipierre · 9 July 2024
Follow-up to the LAION-5B finding, examining who bears responsibility when children's images end up in AI training data. Confirms images were taken from places people assumed were private, including school websites and personal blogs. LAION's position is that responsibility sits with whoever published to the public internet.
3 July 2024
Photos of Australian children used in dataset to train AI, human rights group says
The Guardian Australia · Josh Taylor · 3 July 2024
Human Rights Watch found 190 photographs of Australian children in the LAION-5B dataset used to train AI image generators including Stable Diffusion and Midjourney. Researcher Hye Jung Han told Guardian Australia the images were taken from photo and video sharing sites as well as school websites, including pages schools had assumed were effectively private because they were unlinked and not indexed by Google.
3 July 2024
Photos of Australian kids have been found in a massive AI training data set. What can we do?
The Conversation · Katharine Kemp, UNSW · 3 July 2024
Legal analysis of the LAION-5B finding focused on what Australian privacy law can and cannot do about children's images being taken into AI training data. References schools generally as institutions that photograph children rather than as a confirmed dataset source.
3 July 2024
Personal photos of Australian children secretly used to train AI
SBS News · Sydney Lang · 3 July 2024
An SBS News In Depth episode on the Human Rights Watch finding. The episode is explicit that a significant share of the images came from school websites, from sections schools had used to share event photographs with parents and believed were not publicly accessible, and that some images carried full names, ages and school information.
3 July 2024
Photos of Aussie kids scraped, used by popular AI tool
Bendigo Advertiser (AAP) · Jennifer Dudley-Nicholson, AAP · 3 July 2024
AAP wire coverage of the Human Rights Watch finding that photographs of Australian children were taken into the LAION-5B dataset. Notes that some images came from restricted sources including unlisted YouTube videos and school websites, used without parental consent, and that removing images from a dataset does not undo their use in already-trained models.
3 July 2024
AI is scanning children's photos. Should you be worried?
ABC Radio Sydney · Angela Catterns, with Ange Lavoipierre · 3 July 2024
A Sydney Breakfast interview on the discovery of roughly 190 images of Australian children in an AI training dataset and what AI systems may do with that data. The segment does not name school websites as a source.
3 July 2024
Children's images being used to train AI
ABC Radio (AM) · Ange Lavoipierre · 3 July 2024
An AM segment on the Human Rights Watch finding that Australian children's photographs, names, locations and ages were taken into the LAION-5B dataset without consent. Hye Jung Han describes finding a newborn and girls in swimsuits at a school sports carnival, and notes that once models have trained on the images the harm cannot be reversed.
3 July 2024
The world's biggest AI models were trained using images of Australian kids, and their families had no idea
ABC News · Ange Lavoipierre · 3 July 2024
Reports the Human Rights Watch finding of 190 identifiable Australian children in the LAION-5B dataset used to train Stable Diffusion and Midjourney. The images carried identifying detail including names, locations and ages, and were sourced from YouTube, Flickr, blogging platforms and school websites. Removing links from the dataset does not remove what trained models have already learned.
29 June 2024
Inside the AI ‘deepnude’ apps infiltrating Australian schools
The Age · David Swan · 29 June 2024
An investigation into nudify apps and their use against Australian schoolchildren. Central to the school-imagery question is the eSafety Commissioner's explanation that offenders no longer need many source images and can simply harvest them from social media.
15 March 2024
Do photos of kids belong online? The photo etiquette rules to remember
The Sydney Morning Herald · Nell Geraets · 15 March 2024
An explainer on photo etiquette for children's images quoting ARC Centre of Excellence for the Digital Child researchers, including a section on photographs taken at schools and sports clubs and how parents can request removal or blurring of uniforms and identifying features.
16 July 2021
School posts on Facebook could threaten student privacy
The Conversation · Joshua Rosenberg, University of Tennessee · 16 July 2021
Reports a large-scale study of school Facebook pages which found schools routinely publish photographs of identifiable students alongside their full names, visible to anyone including data miners and facial recognition firms. The underlying dataset is United States schools, not Australian, though the piece ran in The Conversation's Australian edition. It remains the landmark quantification of this practice.
4 December 2019
Digital privacy: What principals need to know in 2020
The Educator K/12 · Brett Henebery · 4 December 2019
Early trade coverage of school image-sharing and consent practice, reporting that schools were receiving increasing parent complaints about photo sharing and consent procedures, and advising schools to centralise communications through their own channels rather than relying primarily on third-party social media.
19 May 2019
Consent to social media or sit out: NSW public schools' ultimatum to students over big events
The Sydney Morning Herald · Pallavi Singhal · 19 May 2019
Landmark early coverage of NSW Department of Education permission to publish practice. Forms for major departmental events were changed from a separate opt-in for name and photo publication to a blanket acknowledgement that participation includes publication to social media. Wollongong parents who objected were told there was no other option than a blanket rule. A privacy expert warns the approach risks scrutiny from the state privacy body.
22 March 2018
How to keep your students' digital information safe
The Educator K/12 · Brett Henebery · 22 March 2018
Argues principals underestimate the privacy implications of publishing student images. Colin Anson describes founding pixevety after his eight-year-old daughter's photographs were published by her school without parental consent. Frames posting student images on social media as a potential breach risk under the Australian Privacy Principles.
18 August 2016
Photos of schoolgirls not child porn, say police
The Australian · Mark Schliebs · 18 August 2016
Pre-AI coverage of the image-harvesting risk. A website solicited and published photographs of girls from more than 70 Australian schools, obtained from social media sites and across the internet, without the girls' knowledge. Queensland Police said the site did not appear to contain child exploitation material. NSW Police contacted the state Education Department.
How this list is maintained

Nothing is listed here on the strength of a search result. Each item was opened, read, and checked for its publication date and byline before it was added, and each link is re-checked before this page is republished. Where a date or a byline could not be confirmed, the entry says so on the page rather than guessing.

Coverage is compiled from a ledger held in the research repository, and this page is generated from that ledger rather than edited by hand, so the page and the record cannot drift apart.
